# How to talk about it: Pietrolino's Secret

**Why this topic at 8–10.** This is the season finale: Pietrolino says goodbye to Emma and goes back to being a pebble of the wood. At this age children face their first important goodbyes (a friend who moves away, a change of school, a grandparent who is no longer here, a pet) and look for a way to give a place to what they feel. The story shows that you can be sad, cry, be comforted, and then keep with you what you've learned. That's why it is only read together.

## Three questions after the story

1. Why does Pietrolino say Emma won't lose him?
2. What did Grandma do when Emma was crying?
3. Is there someone, or something, that taught you something you still do today?

## In real life

If the child is going through a goodbye, you can choose together a gesture or an object that recalls it (a drawing, a photo, something to do the way that person did it). Leave room for tears without rushing to end them: like Grandma, sometimes just staying close without talking is enough. If the child asks whether Pietrolino "died", you can answer that he went back to his place in the wood, and then ask the child what they think.

## Common mistakes

- Saying "don't cry" or "come on, it's nothing": the sadness of a goodbye is right.
- Using the story to talk about a recent bereavement without first reading it on your own: consider whether it's the right moment.
- Filling the silence with explanations: often being there is enough.
