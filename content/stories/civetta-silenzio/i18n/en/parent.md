# How to talk about it: The Owl of Silence

**Why this topic at 6–7.** Children today live surrounded by sound: voices, screens, background noise. Silence can seem empty or even frightening. The story turns it into a full experience, shared with an adult, and links it to a simple way to calm down: listening to your own breathing.

## Three questions after the story

1. What did Emma do when she couldn't sleep?
2. What did the owl listen to at night?
3. When you close your eyes and stay silent, what can you hear?

## In real life

Try the "minute of silence" before bed or at a hectic moment: eyes closed, listen to your breathing, then everyone tells one sound they heard. If the child can't sleep, welcome them as Grandma does: without getting cross, calmly, and then back to bed.

## Common mistakes

- Using silence as a punishment ("now be quiet").
- Expecting a long silence: a minute is already a lot at this age.
- Dismissing sleeplessness with "just go to sleep": first welcome it, then slow down.
