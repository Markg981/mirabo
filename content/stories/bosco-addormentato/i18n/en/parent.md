# How to talk about it: The Sleeping Wood

**Why this topic at 6–7.** Many children experience sleep as a loss ("I'll miss the games") or link it to the sadness of the dark. The story presents it as something all of nature does, to grow and start again, and shows that stopping is not giving up.

## Three questions after the story

1. Why does the wood fall asleep in winter?
2. What did the hedgehog do before sleeping?
3. What helps you fall asleep?

## In real life

Build a little bedtime ritual together that stays the same every evening: three slow breaths, a sentence to say goodbye to the day, the light going down. If there are dry leaves in the garden, leave a pile in a corner: it can become a hedgehog's shelter. Wild animals are watched, not touched.

## Common mistakes

- Using sleep as a punishment ("if you're naughty you go to bed").
- Saying the wood "dies" in winter: it sleeps, and in spring it wakes up (episode 9).
- Expecting the child to fall asleep straight away: the ritual is for slowing down, not for timing.
