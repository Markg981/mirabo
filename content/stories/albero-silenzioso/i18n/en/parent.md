# How to talk about it: The Tree That Stopped Talking

**Why this topic at 5–7.** Children understand well that a living thing can feel unwell too. The story shows that taking care is made of small, repeated actions (cleaning up, planting, staying close), not a magic gesture, and that results come with time.

## Three questions after the story

1. What did Emma do before planting the seeds?
2. Why does Pietrolino say "not with force, with care"?
3. Is there something, or someone, you'd like to take care of?

## In real life

Plant a seed in a little pot together (beans or lentils sprout in a few days) and decide who waters it and when. If you find litter in a park, pick it up only with gloves and a bag, and agree together on what must not be touched.

## Common mistakes

- Promising "it'll come up tomorrow": seeds take their time, and waiting is part of the story.
- Doing all the planting yourself.
- Looking for someone to blame for the damaged tree: the story is about what can be done, not who did wrong.
