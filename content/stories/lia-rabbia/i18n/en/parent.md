# How to talk about it: Lia's Volcano

**Why this topic at 5–7.** At this age anger still arrives all at once and children don't yet have the tools to stop it on their own; it shouldn't be removed, it should be recognised and accompanied. A name and an image (the volcano) and a gesture (the breath) give them something to do while it passes.

## Three questions after the story

1. Where did Lia feel the volcano? And where do you feel it when you get angry?
2. Do you think Grandma was right not to scold Lia? Why?
3. Was there a time when your volcano erupted? What would have helped you?

## In real life

When the volcano rises, name it ("I can see you're really angry") instead of stopping it ("calm down"); do the breath **together**, don't order it; talk about what happened only afterwards, once the volcano has gone down. Repair (tidying up, rebuilding, saying sorry) comes later, and works better if the child suggests it.

## Common mistakes

- "There's nothing to be angry about": for them there is, and they feel it.
- Punishing the anger instead of the action: being angry is allowed, hitting is not.
- Fixing things for them (rebuilding the tower): it takes away the repair.
- Demanding the breath once it has already erupted: the gesture is learnt when calm and used when it rises.

## If something worries you

Anger that very often hurts themselves or others, fear of an adult: listen without questioning, and talk to your paediatrician or their teachers. The app sees only a small part of their day.
