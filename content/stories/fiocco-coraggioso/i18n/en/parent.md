# How to talk about it: The Snowflake That Didn't Want to Fall

**Why this topic at 6–7.** Starting school brings the first challenges (reading, writing, team games) and with them the fear of getting things wrong. Some children would rather not try than make a mistake. The snowflake gives this fear a name and shows that you can be scared and still have a go.

## Three questions after the story

1. What was the snowflake afraid of?
2. What did Emma say to help it?
3. Is there something you'd like to try, but it scares you a bit?

## In real life

Tell them first about a mistake you made and how it turned out: for a child, hearing that grown-ups get things wrong too is very reassuring. When something new makes them freeze, help them do just the first little bit ("just write the first letter") and praise the attempt, not the result.

## Common mistakes

- Saying "come on, there's nothing to be scared of": the fear is real and needs to be welcomed first.
- Correcting every mistake immediately, taking away the fun of trying again.
- Praising only when it goes well ("well done, perfect") instead of when they try.
