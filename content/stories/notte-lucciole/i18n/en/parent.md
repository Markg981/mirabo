# How to talk about it: The Night of the Fireflies

**Why this topic at 6–7.** Many children are afraid of the dark at this age. The story doesn't deny the fear: it puts it next to curiosity and a hand to hold, and shows that being in the dark with a trusted adult can become a moment of wonder.

## Three questions after the story

1. What happened when Emma turned off the lantern?
2. Who was next to Emma in the dark?
3. What's the most beautiful thing you've ever seen in the evening or at night?

## In real life

One evening, turn off the light in the room together, holding hands, and count slowly to twenty: your eyes get used to it and things appear that you couldn't see before (the moon, distant lights, stars). If you live near meadows or woods, in June and July you can go looking for fireflies, with torches switched off.

## Common mistakes

- Saying "there's nothing to be afraid of": the fear is there, you can only stay close to it.
- Forcing the dark if the child isn't ready: a small light can stay on.
- Turning the wonder into a science lesson straight afterwards.
