# How to talk about it: The Little Rebel Raindrop

**Why this topic at 5–7.** Tap water seems endless, and at this age you can't see where it comes from or where it goes. Giving a voice to a raindrop makes an everyday action (turning off the tap) concrete, and shows a simple idea of the water cycle: rain goes up, comes back down, changes shape.

## Three questions after the story

1. Why was Drop jumping against the current?
2. What did Emma promise, and what did she do when she got home?
3. When do we use water at home?

## In real life

Put a little bucket or bowl outside when it rains and use that water for the plants: the child sees how much falls. While you brush your teeth together, have a race to see who remembers to turn off the tap. For the curious ones: a closed jar with a little water in the sun shows the droplets "going up".

## Common mistakes

- Making the child feel responsible for droughts or the planet's problems.
- Turning every wash into a check ("you wasted it again").
- Promising that drops are "never wasted": the story says they can end up in pipes without being used.
