# How to talk about it: The Wood Wakes Up

**Why this topic at 5–7.** Having watched the wood fall asleep, the child finds it awake again but hurt. The story stresses two things: dangerous things (rusty cans, glass) are handled by an adult wearing gloves, and wild animals are helped without picking them up. And it takes caring for the wood to school, with classmates: alone you can do something, together you can do more.

## Three questions after the story

1. Why didn't Emma pick the hedgehog up?
2. Who picked up the can, and why?
3. What would you write on a sign for your park?

## In real life

Leave a pile of leaves and twigs in a corner of the garden or yard: it's a shelter for hedgehogs and insects. Teach the golden rule: a wild animal is watched, not touched; if it looks hurt, call an adult (and, if needed, a wildlife rescue centre). Draw a sign for the park together, or suggest it to the teacher as a class project.

## Common mistakes

- Blaming the litter on "people": the story only says someone left a can and that it can be put right.
- Letting the child pick up sharp or rusty objects.
- Promising that "a sign is enough": care is continuous, as the fox says.
